# Six levels of AI-native collective productivity

**Apoco, September 2026.** Six levels, **0–5**, including the baseline.

**Start with the [reader guide](reader-guide.md)** for the overview, examples at each level and a placement exercise. This document holds the detailed conditions, company examples and evidence rules. The examples show possible ways to reach each gain; they are not a capability checklist or observed outcomes.

## Subject and scope

**How well does the organization combine human intelligence with AI to accomplish worthwhile work, reduce avoidable effort and develop what it can accomplish next?**

AI’s contribution must be identifiable. The productive capability belongs to people and AI working together: human expertise, initiative, relationships and judgment remain part of it. We use **AI** when describing the technology’s contribution and **collective intelligence** for their combined ability to understand, decide, create and learn. More capable AI alone does not establish greater organizational productivity.

For this draft, the assessed entity is the same organization at every level: a team, department or company responsible for a stated set of outcomes. “Organization” means that unit throughout. Higher levels require stronger collective benefit within that scope; a team does not become a company as it progresses. A team assessment includes consequences for the wider organization. A company assessment cannot be inferred from one strong team.

**In this guide, productivity means useful results for the full effort, time, attention and cost they take, including review and rework.** Quality, value, total resources, elapsed time and development are part of the level claims. Tools, agents, autonomy and routines explain possible mechanisms. They do not earn a level. The framework addresses AI-enabled productivity and effectiveness; it does not rank unrelated companies by revenue or imply that a low-AI organization is generally ineffective. **AI-native** means designing work around useful collaboration between people and AI.

## The six proposed levels

| Level | Productive situation | What has to be different in actual work |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **No useful AI-enabled gain yet.** | The organization may already work very well. AI is not used in the work being considered, or examination of its use shows no useful gain after full costs. Unknown effects remain unassigned. |
| **1** | **AI produces useful gains in parts of the work.** | People create, understand or decide better, faster or with less effort. These gains survive checking at that point, but their effect on the complete organizational result is limited or unestablished. |
| **2** | **AI improves complete, worthwhile results.** | A customer or internal recipient gets a result that meets the actual need, with better quality, less total effort, shorter elapsed time, or newly practical capability at justified cost. The gain survives the handoffs, review and rework needed to finish it. |
| **3** | **AI-enabled collaboration increases what the organization can accomplish together.** | AI improves how people combine knowledge, judgment and contributions in shared investigation, creation, decisions and delivery. The organization accomplishes more valuable or demanding work within its resources. This can happen within one project or across connected commitments. Benefits remain after displaced work, attention, chasing, support and maintenance costs are included. |
| **4** | **AI helps the organization keep improving how it works.** | AI contributes to improvements in methods and people’s capability. Recorded comparisons across successive work cycles show better results, less avoidable effort or greater practical reach that justifies the investment in learning and change. Measurement is required throughout; this level adds repeated demonstrated improvement in the work. Failed experiments and uneven cycles remain in the record. The improvement is dependable rather than reliant on occasional heroic follow-up. |
| **5** | **Strategy and delivery improve each other.** | People and AI help choose worthwhile opportunities, develop the capabilities they need and carry choices into valuable results within useful time and resource limits. Changed direction reaches affected work; delivery evidence improves strategic choices and stops poor investments. These two-way benefits hold across important outcomes and dependencies, including when direction changes. Useful delivery and people’s development continue, with little attention lost to routine chasing. |

Each level adds a more demanding productive condition: **local benefit → complete-result benefit → collective benefit → sustained improvement → strategic renewal and accomplishment.** Level 0 supplies the baseline. These are ordinal descriptions, not equal numerical steps or guaranteed stages of adoption.

Good judgment, learning, purpose and the ability to disagree belong throughout. The upper levels require evidence that intelligence helps sustain their productive effects across the assessed work. They do not claim these qualities first appear late. A technically simple arrangement can qualify; elaborate agents with poor results cannot.

## Useful AI proactivity

The operating examples make useful AI initiative explicit, especially at the upper levels: AI notices a relevant change, understands the implications, acts within its remit or brings a concrete decision, follows through and checks the result. People do not have to prompt every step. Notifications and automatic activity alone do not establish the benefit. Proactivity can appear earlier; its productive effect across the assessed work matters, not a tool’s autonomy setting. Include unnecessary actions, interruptions, correction and supervision in the cost.

**Productive benefit decides placement; agent initiation is not a separate gate.** A human-initiated AI-assisted routine can qualify when the same collective gains hold at the same total burden. Dependence on repeated chasing matters through its cost and fragility. Proactive agents remain a useful mechanism to test, not a substitute for outcome evidence.

## What cumulative means

**Each higher level retains or improves the positive productive conditions below it.** Level 0 is the starting baseline, not a condition to retain. The limitations described at earlier levels are overcome, not inherited. Useful contributions survive into complete results; complete results work together; collective accomplishment improves through learning; growing capability supports greater strategic accomplishment. A higher-level claim cannot compensate for unreliable useful delivery with impressive learning routines or strategy presentations.

This is cumulative benefit, not cumulative machinery. A company can replace tools, remove meetings or abandon an earlier workflow while retaining its gains. It need not demonstrate a separate personal AI assistant for every employee. Human conversation, craft and judgment can remain the best way to do particular work. Nor must every metric improve at once: investments and trade-offs need a stated time horizon and worthwhile results.

These are conditions, not a mandatory adoption itinerary. An organization may establish several together. Higher-level patterns in one pocket do not erase gaps elsewhere in the assessed scope. A useful strategic decision alone is not a level-5 pattern. A CEO using a personal assistant to examine an investment may achieve a local gain in judgment; the decision’s subject or importance does not determine placement. Level 5 requires a dependable connection between delivery evidence, strategic choices, action on affected commitments and subsequent results across the assessed work. One successful decision or one complete cycle does not establish that condition. Record isolated successes as value; identify a stronger pattern only when its operating conditions and productive effects are supported, while keeping gaps elsewhere visible. Choose an improvement by the real constraint, even when that means testing a strategic choice before refining a workflow.

## Recognition, skill and engineering

The [reader guide](reader-guide.md) pairs each level with a “Not yet” condition and typical “Enabled by” setups. “Not yet” means the next productive condition is unestablished. It does not require lower levels to lack learning or strategic thought. At level 3, collective gains can last without demonstrated continuing improvement; at level 4, strategy may be questioned without a dependable two-way strategic benefit.

Skill in using AI (“taste”) is a mechanism throughout: defining good work, delegating, checking against sources, knowing when to trust or stop AI, and retaining the ability to handle consequential judgments and failures. Good practice must improve complete results and become usable by colleagues. Continuing development includes people’s own capability, not only better assisted output. At the strategic level, it includes judging which investments and uses of AI deserve resources.

Attention is a cost to inspect throughout. Switching between sessions, reconstructing context and checking parallel outputs can consume local gains. Shared agents, useful follow-up and selective requests for decisions can reduce that burden. Their effect must be checked; a higher level does not guarantee fewer interruptions for every person.

Engineering enables, never earns, the gain. Off-the-shelf tools can support demanding work when they have appropriate context, access, skills and permissions. Typical setups range from connected assistants to agents carrying defined processes, shared team agents and linked work-outcome records. People sign off on consequential choices; routine actions can proceed within an agreed remit. More access needs more control. Sandboxing is a security choice, and sovereignty depends on the work’s sensitivity and applicable obligations at any level. Specific technologies and private models are not level requirements.

Concrete setups make the productive situations easier to recognize:

| Situation | What people and AI might do | What still needs to be established |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Copy material into a chat, move the answer back and check it manually. | No useful gain after full effort, or no AI use in the stated scope. Unknown effects leave placement open; manual use can also support positive levels. |
| **1** | Search project documents, work with repository context and shared instructions, or run an analysis over data. | A useful local result after checking; access alone proves nothing about benefit. |
| **2** | Prepare a pull request with tests, write a report where colleagues use it, or build a temporary tool to test a hypothesis. | Completion against the actual task and useful results at justified total cost, including approval effort. |
| **3** | Work with shared agents and context to investigate a problem, develop a proposal or carry agreed decisions into action together. | Better shared work after full costs. Multiplayer access is an enabler; cross-project coordination is one example. |
| **4** | Examine recurring review failures, test a change to guidance or practice, and compare subsequent work. | Repeated improvements in results and people’s capability, beyond better agent evaluations or more reports. |
| **5** | Connect delivery problems to a strategic assumption, bring evidence for a decision, and carry the changed direction into affected commitments. | Better strategic choices and worthwhile delivery in both directions, within the same assessed scope. |

These are illustrative mechanisms. A team could have every feature in the level 3 example and lose its gains to alerts, corrections and supervision. Another could use a simpler assistant to resolve the same conflicts effectively. The useful combined result and full effort decide placement.

Agent telemetry and evaluations can help diagnose failures. Level 4 needs evidence about the work itself, such as cycle time, rework, repeat problems, quality and demonstrated skill. Improving an agent’s score alone does not establish a more productive organization.

## One company operating at each level

**Hypothetical operating portraits, not measured results or claims about Apoco.** The same company sells, configures and introduces customer-support systems for client businesses, trains their staff and supports them afterward. Success means clients can handle their customers’ enquiries well, at worthwhile cost—not merely that software was installed. Sales, delivery, training, support and leadership remain in scope at every level. Each portrait carries forward the gains below it.

### 0 — No useful AI-enabled gain yet

The company may run a good business. Sales consults specialists before making promises; delivery configures the service; trainers prepare the client; support handles problems. People maintain shared plans and discuss priorities. Some try AI for emails or setup questions. Comparing the results and full effort, including checking and correction, shows no useful gain in the work examined. If that comparison had not been made, the effect would remain unknown and would not support a level-0 placement.

**Productive difference:** no useful AI-enabled gain in the work examined. No AI use in the assessed scope also belongs at level 0. This says nothing by itself about the company’s overall competence.

**What comes next:** find a real constraint where AI can help and establish whether the resulting work is actually better, faster or less demanding.

### 1 — AI produces useful gains in parts of the work

A salesperson uses AI to compare a client’s requirements with previous proposals. An implementation specialist uses it to explore configuration options. A trainer develops clearer examples with AI, then checks them against the client’s work. People spend less effort on these tasks or produce more useful work; those local benefits survive review.

The company still assembles the whole engagement through its existing processes. A better proposal might leave delivery with the same clarification burden. Support may still discover that the client misunderstood a feature. Individual gains have not yet established a better journey from request to successful use.

**Productive difference:** worthwhile local gains. Their effect on the full customer result remains limited or unknown.

**What comes next:** follow an engagement through delivery, use and support to find where those gains survive or disappear.

### 2 — AI improves complete, worthwhile results

For an engagement, people and AI carry the client’s needs through proposal, configuration, testing and training. An agent flags a promise the configuration does not meet; a specialist resolves it before the client trains staff. AI helps prepare realistic test cases and tailored guidance. It can build a temporary test tool when that makes a previously impractical check feasible. A prepared report or pull request completes the assignment only if that is the agreed endpoint; review, implementation and successful use remain part of a task that requires them. People work directly with the client where understanding and trust matter.

The client can use the service successfully, with less combined effort, a shorter useful delivery time or better quality at justified cost. Checking, corrections and early support are included. Colleagues still contribute or review through their usual handoffs; an improvement in how they combine their knowledge and judgment is not yet established. Competing engagements may also remain difficult to reconcile.

**Productive difference:** the gain reaches a complete, worthwhile customer result. An AI-enabled gain in how people accomplish shared work is not yet established.

**What comes next:** choose work needing several people’s expertise; test whether working with shared AI context improves the result and reduces reconstruction or rework.

### 3 — AI-enabled collaboration increases what the organization can accomplish together

Sales, delivery, training and support work with shared agents and context to develop an engagement. They examine requirements and test possible solutions together. A trainer identifies an assumption that would make the proposed configuration difficult for client staff to use. AI connects that concern to the design, prepares alternatives and carries the agreed change into the proposal, configuration and training materials. People contribute expertise and challenge the options; the agent helps them continue from the same understanding.

The company produces more useful solutions with less reconstruction, late correction or senior rescue. The improvement depends on the combined contributions of people and AI, with review and context upkeep included. Multiplayer AI makes this way of working practical, but shared access alone does not establish the result. Personal agents can continue to support individual work.

That shared participation also helps across engagements. Before sales promises a difficult feature, agents help expose what it would displace and what support it would require. People reshape or decline the request, and agents carry agreed changes into affected plans. The gain remains worthwhile after interruptions, supervision and costs transferred to other work are counted.

**Productive difference:** AI improves how the company’s people investigate, create, decide and deliver together. This can appear within one project or across commitments. Reusing shared knowledge can help already; repeated improvement in methods, people’s capability and later results remains to be established at level 4.

**What comes next:** make experience reliably improve subsequent work and develop more people’s ability to handle it.

### 4 — AI helps the organization keep improving how it works

Briefs draw on relevant lessons from earlier engagements. During work, AI helps people examine difficult decisions and practise unfamiliar tasks. Debriefs compare what was expected with what happened. Agents surface recurring causes, help turn agreed lessons into changes, and check whether those changes helped later clients. People challenge the interpretation rather than accepting an automated verdict.

For example, repeated training difficulties lead to a better way to discover client needs, more effective preparation and practice for newer specialists. The later work demonstrates the benefit: fewer repeated problems, better judgment spread across more people, or demanding implementations becoming practical without constant senior rescue. These gains justify the time spent learning. Agents help sustain the habits without turning them into more administrative work. Some changes fail or take time to help; compare repeated work over a stated period rather than requiring every cycle or metric to improve.

**Productive difference:** the company repeatedly increases its ability to do useful work; it does not merely repeat a successful operating pattern.

**What comes next:** connect this growing capability to decisions about which opportunities and services the company should pursue, change or stop.

### 5 — Strategy and delivery improve each other

Client evidence, delivery experience and market changes inform an ongoing view of where the company can create value. People and AI challenge assumptions and compare opportunities with the company’s purpose, capabilities and resources.

For example, the company is pursuing larger clients. AI connects repeated implementation delays and support demands to assumptions behind that strategy. People examine the evidence and decide to narrow the target segment. Agents identify affected sales opportunities, delivery plans and training needs, carry through the agreed changes and track whether later engagements yield better results at worthwhile cost. A promising new service can also be tested with clients; a weak opportunity is declined or stopped. People develop the expertise and relationships needed to make a worthwhile opportunity real.

When the direction changes, agents help translate it into compatible sales commitments, delivery plans, training and support. Each person receives the implications relevant to their work, with the reasons and uncertainties, rather than another company-wide information dump. Routine follow-through happens without repeated chasing; important conflicts, surprises and decisions reach people in time. Human attention goes into discovery, difficult choices, creative work and relationships.

The new service must produce worthwhile customer and business results within useful time and resource limits while existing commitments remain well served. Learning from it improves both the offering and the company’s capacity for the next opportunity. Strategic accomplishment is visible in what the company achieves and stops wasting effort on, not in the fluency of its plans.

**Productive difference:** an increasingly capable company keeps choosing and realizing valuable directions, while maintaining useful delivery and developing its people.

**What comes next:** keep testing the value of its choices and the limits of its capability. The highest level is a continuing condition to sustain, not the end of improvement.

The destination is a well-functioning human–agent organization: valuable intent becomes useful accomplishment without people repeatedly rebuilding the coordination around it. It is a proposed ambition, not a six-to-twelve-month forecast or a claim of unlimited productivity. The examples illustrate possible mechanisms; they do not prescribe a software stack or prove that the level boundaries are valid.

## Why the boundaries might be useful

| Transition | Different question and next move |
|---|---|
| **0 → 1** | Establish a useful gain on a real constraint, compared with the current approach. |
| **1 → 2** | Follow the gain through to use. Remove the review, rework or handoff that erases it. |
| **2 → 3** | Examine how people combine knowledge, judgment and contributions. Test shared human–AI work within a project or across commitments, and check the resulting collective gain after full costs. |
| **3 → 4** | Show how repeated work develops people and methods, and whether subsequent results justify that effort. Sustain a useful improvement beyond its initiator. |
| **4 → 5** | Connect growing capability and evidence to strategic choices, then show that changed priorities become valuable results at acceptable time and cost. |

The hardest boundaries remain **2/3** and **4/5**. Complete delivery may already include improved human–AI collaboration; improvement may already include strategic renewal. In those cases do not manufacture a distinction or assign a higher level by rhetoric. Test whether each boundary changes the diagnosis and next action. The count must yield to evidence if the distinctions fail.

## Evidence without false precision

The reader guide’s [Find your starting point exercise](reader-guide.md#find-your-starting-point) turns these requirements into a provisional placement and a practical test. Its [worked example](reader-guide.md#worked-example-from-individual-delivery-to-shared-work) applies the 2/3 distinction within a stated scope. It is a hypothetical illustration, not outcome evidence or a validated scoring method. Record supported conditions, gaps, the change to try, its owner, comparison and review date.

Evidence is required at every positive level. Level 4 specifically requires recorded comparisons across repeated work; useful rituals or a feeling of progress do not establish continuing improvement. Measurement can combine time, effort, rework and recurrence data with supported judgments of quality, use and skills demonstrated in practice. A dashboard alone is not evidence of causation.

For each claimed gain identify the useful result, assessed work, time period, credible baseline or alternative, and what AI contributed. Examine quality and use, total human effort, AI/tool and upkeep costs, elapsed time including waiting, attention lost to interruptions and context reconstruction, and effects on other work. Include unsuccessful cases, not only demonstrations. Distinguish useful exploration from avoidable rework rather than minimizing iteration count.

For levels 4 and 5, also examine subsequent cycles: changed skill or judgment, reduced recurring problems, newly practical work, and decisions that affected actual commitments. Learning investments can take time; state the horizon and interim evidence rather than treating future benefit as already achieved. A more valuable result can justify greater effort or time, but that trade-off must be explicit.

Company coverage rules and numeric thresholds are still open. Identify pockets, gaps and unknown dependencies; do not average team numbers or infer a company level from one outcome. A high-level claim without adequate coverage remains unestablished. The evidence supports or challenges the level itself, rather than supplying a separate optional productivity score.

### Reporting mixed evidence

**Hypothetical illustration.** A service department examines onboarding and ongoing support over the same period. Records support AI-enabled improvements in complete results after full costs in both services. Onboarding also shows better shared investigation through AI; that condition is unestablished in support. A useful report is: “Provisional level 2 across the assessed services, with level-3 conditions supported in onboarding.” It preserves the stronger result without assigning it to all the work.

If support has not been examined at all, even the department-wide level 2 remains open. Narrow the claim to onboarding or gather the missing evidence. Missing evidence for a higher level does not establish a lower one. Examine common work, difficult exceptions and consequential dependencies; a successful demonstration is not coverage of the department.

Count costs to everyone affected rather than requiring every person's effort to fall. More trainer preparation may be worthwhile if it prevents greater client rework at the required quality. State that trade-off and include the added effort; moving an uncounted burden to someone else cannot establish a gain.

## Why six, and what the midpoint research does not establish

Six includes level 0, so the numbered range 0–5 has no single central category. Four would compress distinctions we still need to examine; eight would currently require additional boundaries we have not earned. This is a design judgment, not a psychometric finding.

Pew discusses avoiding neutral midpoints when they invite easy answers, while noting that midpoint responses can also reflect genuine neutrality or insufficient knowledge. Its example concerns attitudes and differs from an ordered capability assessment. Therefore removing our middle category does not establish reduced bias or more accurate placement. [Pew's methodological account](https://www.pewresearch.org/decoded/2021/03/04/what-different-survey-modes-and-question-types-can-tell-us-about-americans-views-of-china/).

Place the organization from concrete evidence rather than asking whether it feels average. Allow **insufficient evidence** and report **mixed conditions** outside the numbered scale; neither is a hidden midpoint. Level 0 covers no AI use or examined use with no useful gain after full costs. If AI is used but its effect is unknown, leave placement open. Establishing an existing benefit through measurement changes what is known; progress requires a change in the work or its results. A range or a supported lower condition with stronger pockets is preferable to false precision. Keep 0 visible: removing it from the presentation would leave five positive levels and recreate a single middle option among those.

## Status

The research cited in the [reader guide](reader-guide.md#sources-and-limits) informs the benefit criteria and development mechanisms. We keep refining the levels as teams use them, especially the boundaries between levels 2 and 3 and between levels 4 and 5.
